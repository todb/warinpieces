71. Pierre had in fact succeeded in getting into a political conversation with the abbe on the balance of power, and the abbe, evidently interested by the simple-hearted fervour of the young man, was unfolding to him his cherished idea.
72. Both were listening and talking too eagerly and naturally, and Anna Pavlovna did not like it.
73. 'The means? — the balance of power in Europe and the rights of the people,'
74. said the abbe.
75. 'One powerful state like Russia — with the prestige of barbarism — need only take a disinterested stand at the head of the alliance that aims at securing the balance of power in Europe, and it would save the world!'
76. 'How are you going to get such a balance of power?'
77. Pierre was beginning; but at that moment Anna Pavlovna came up, and glancing severely at Pierre, asked the Italian how he was supporting the climate.
78. The Italian's face changed instantly and assumed the look of offensive, affected sweetness, which was evidently its habitual expression in conversation with women.
79. 'I am so enchanted by the wit and culture of the society — especially of the ladies — in which I have had the happiness to be received, that I have not yet had time to think of the climate,'
80. he said.
81. Not letting the abbe and Pierre slip out of her grasp, Anna Pavlovna, for greater convenience in watching them, made them join the bigger group.
82. At that moment another guest walked into the drawing-room.
83. This was the young Prince Andrey Bolkonsky, the husband of the little princess.
84. Prince Bolkonsky was a very handsome young man, of medium height, with clear, clean-cut features.
85. Everything in his appearance, from his weary, bored expression to his slow, measured step, formed the most striking contrast to his lively little wife.
86. Obviously all the people in the drawing-room were familiar figures to him, and more than that, he was unmistakably so sick of them that even to look at them and to listen to them was a weariness to him.
87. Of all the wearisome faces the face of his pretty wife seemed to bore him most.
88. With a grimace that distorted his handsome face he turned away from her.
89. He kissed Anna Pavlovna's hand, and with half-closed eyelids scanned the whole company.
90. 'You are enlisting for the war, prince?'
91. said Anna Pavlovna.
92. 'General Kutuzov has been kind enough to have me as an aide-de-camp,'
93. said Bolkonsky.
94. 'And Lise, your wife?'
95. 'She is going into the country.'
96. 'Isn't it too bad of you to rob us of your charming wife?'
97. 'Andre,'
98. said his wife, addressing her husband in exactly the same coquettish tone in which she spoke to outsiders,
99. 'the vicomte has just told us such a story about Mlle. Georges and Bonaparte!'
100. Prince Andrey scowled and turned away.
101. Pierre, who had kept his eyes joyfully and affectionately fixed on him ever since he came in, went up to him and took hold of his arm.
102. Prince Andrey, without looking round, twisted his face into a grimace of annoyance at any one's touching him, but seeing Pierre's smiling face, he gave him a smile that was unexpectedly sweet and pleasant.
